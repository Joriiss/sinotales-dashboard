Weather: Mid-March marks the beginning of spring in Beijing, with temperatures quickly rising from around zero to 3–15°C (37-59°F). Mornings and evenings can still be chilly, and the air is dry, often accompanied by strong winds. Occasionally, there might be sandstorms, but these usually last only half a day or a day and generally do not affect sightseeing. If you encounter significant sandstorms, you could opt to visit indoor attractions, such as a hutong home visit. What to Wear: A sweater and jacket are usually sufficient, but when visiting the Great Wall, you might want to bring a hat, gloves, and windproof pants to avoid getting too cold from the wind. During the day in the city, if the weather is warm, a light windbreaker will suffice. Due to the dry air, it's a good idea to bring lip balm, moisturizer, and sunglasses. A mask can be very useful if you encounter a sandstorm. Is March a Good Time to Visit Beijing? March is much more comfortable than winter and is the last low season before the tourist rush begins in April. If you want to avoid the crowds, March is an ideal choice. Although early March can be quite cold, temperatures gradually rise by the end of the month, and the city starts to feel the breath of spring. During this time, spring flowers like peach blossoms, magnolias, and cherry blossoms begin to bloom, transforming the city from bare trees to a vibrant landscape. However, note that after March 15th, indoor heating in Beijing is turned off. Even though daytime temperatures start to rise, it can be quite chilly indoors. You might want to bring a light jacket to stay comfortable both indoors and outdoors. Top Places to Enjoy Flower Blossom In March, the attractions in Beijing are less crowded, allowing for a more comfortable and leisurely visit. Besides must-see landmarks like the Great Wall and the Forbidden City, you could also include spring flower viewing in your itinerary during the middle to late part of the month, adding a touch of relaxation and romance to your Beijing trip. Mutianyu or Jiankou Great Wall • Best time for flower viewing: March 20th - March 31st Compared to the more crowded Badaling, the Mutianyu and Jiankou sections of the Great Wall are less frequented and are great places for flower viewing. The hillsides here are adorned with wild apricot and peach blossoms. Walking along the ancient walls surrounded by the soft pink hues of spring is a wonderful experience. If you prefer an easier visit, Mutianyu is an ideal choice, with cable cars and well-developed facilities. You could take the cable car directly to Tower 14 and then walk towards Towers 15 to 20, where the flowers are usually most abundant. Spring flowers in Mutianyu Jiankou, on the other hand, remains largely unrestored, preserving more of its original appearance. It is a paradise for hikers and photographers. The section from Zhengbeilou to Mutianyu is highly recommended, as it offers the most beautiful scenery, with concentrated flowers providing a particularly enchanting view.
