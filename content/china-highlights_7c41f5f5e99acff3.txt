Geologically and culturally speaking, Hong Kong is a key gateway to Chinese mainland. It is a "halfway house" between West and East, and a diverse blend of multiple cultures. It is very convenient to travel from Hong Kong to the most fascinating destinations in Chinese mainland. Why Go Through Hong Kong? Hong Kong is strategically located at the heart of Asia, conveniently connecting Asia, and especially China, with the rest of the world. 2. A Number of Flights Available There are many flights between Hong Kong and major cities of the US, Australia, and Europe. Hong Kong International Airport is China's second largest airport. Hundreds of flights take off every week between Hong Kong and Chinese mainland's key cities. It is convenient to travel to the Chinese mainland via Hong Kong. 3. Relaxed Visa Policy Hong Kong has one of the most relaxed visa policies in the world. Visitors from approximately 160 countries are allowed to enter Hong Kong without visas. 4. To Enable Visa-Free Transit Access to Chinese Mainland Travel from Hong Kong to Chinese mainland. Many key cities in the Chinese mainland, like Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an, Chengdu, and Guilin, have a 10-day visa–free transit policy. In the policy, Hong Kong is considered as a third region for transit purposes, providing a great opportunity for passengers to take an extension tour to the Chinese mainland, e.g. U.S. – HK – Beijing – U.S. Travel from Hong Kong to Major Cities in Chinese Mainland There are regular flights between Hong Kong and over 40 cities in Chinese mainland, including Beijing, Shanghai, Xi'an, Chengdu, Chongqing, Guilin, and Guangzhou. Most cities can be reached within 5 hours. It is very convenient to travel from Hong Kong to the Chinese mainland. To Number of Direct Flights Per Day (Approx.) Journey Time (Approx.) Make sure that your passport has at least one blank visa page and is valid for at least six months. Nationals of most countries do not require visas to enter Hong Kong. If you are going to visit 10-day visa-free cities, you do not need a visa as long as you meet the policy's criteria. Learn more about the Visa-Free Transit Policy. If you want to explore the cities that are not covered in the 10-day visa-free zones or stay longer than 10 days, we can help you apply for a port visa, eliminating the need for an in-person interview at the embassy. The currency in the Chinese mainland is the Renminbi (RMB). Even though credit cards are accepted in larger establishments in Chinese mainland, cash is necessary for most things. The currency in the mainland is the Renminbi (RMB). You can change money in Hong Kong or in the mainland, or get yuan from ATMs supporting e.g. Visa/Mastercard. See more on our Practical Guide to Chinese Money. 3. Adapters/Converters The standard electrical voltage in Chinese mainland is 220 volts AC, 50 Hz. There are two standard sockets: Type A and Europlug (2-thin-pin and 2-round-pin) and Type I (3-thin-pin). If they are different from those in your country, you need to buy a portable plug adapter for your electronics. They can be easily purchased at most electronic stores, travel supply stores, and airports. See China Power Supply and Adapters for more. 4. One More Thing: an Open Mind China is developing very quickly, and tourism facilities in big cities like Beijing and Shanghai are well-developed, but the conditions in small cities and remote towns can't compare to those of the big cities. It might be noisy in restaurants and there might be long queues for scenic spots… that is the real China.
