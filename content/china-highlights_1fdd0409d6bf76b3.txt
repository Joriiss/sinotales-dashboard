Gansu — the Silk Road's corridor to China — is a must-visit region for a China Silk Road tour. There, deserts, grasslands, snowy mountains, and rainbow mountains complement each other's beauty. Ancient Silk Road culture and Tibetan culture converge. Use this page for inspiration and to get prepared for a Gansu tour. Gansu is the seventh largest province in China and covers an area of 454,000 square kilometers (175,000 square miles). Here are its must-see cities and their key features. The Mingsha Mountains • Dunhuang features Buddhist culture and desert scenery. Suitable for people who are interested in Silk Road culture, the top attraction is the Mogao Caves — a Buddhist and medieval art treasure trove. The pristine dunes and oasis are also very popular. A trip to Dunhuang needs 3–4 days. • Zhangye features stunning landscapes. Suitable for people who like natural scenery and taking photos, the top attraction is Zhangye Danxia National Geopark (The Rainbow Mountains). A trip to Zhangye needs 1–2 days. • Gannan features Tibetan culture and plateau scenery. Suitable for people who like Tibetan environments, the top attractions include Zhagana (a charming Tibetan village) and Labrang Monastery. A trip to Gannan needs 6 days. If you want to explore more of Gansu, we recommend Jiayuguan and Tianshui. In Jiayuguan, you can explore where the Great Wall began in the Ming Dynasty (1368–1644). Tianshui has old Silk Road sites, like the Maiji Mountain Grottoes — one of China's 'Four Major Grottoes'. How Long to Stay in Gansu Typically, a trip to Gansu needs 6 to 12 days according to your interests. Here we suggest itineraries for different durations (contact us to tailor them to suit you). For more weather-related travel information, see Gansu Weather. Lanzhou is the transportation hub of Gansu. Most visitors choose to fly to Lanzhou first and take a bullet train to other cities such as Zhangye, Dunhuang, and Jiayuguan. A few cities have direct flights to Dunhuang and Zhangye, like Beijing, Xi'an, and Shanghai. Bullet trains are the recommended way to travel around most parts of Gansu. If you want to visit the Tibetan autonomous prefecture of Gannan, hiring a private car is the best choice, because there is no railway in Gannan. For more transportation-related travel information, see Gansu Transportation. Gansu people like eating noodles. You will find all kinds of noodles. The most famous is Lanzhou beef noodles. Beef and mutton are their main meats. Hand-grabbed mutton is the specialty. Dunhuang has the most sunlight, so the melons and fruits produced there are particularly sweet, especially the apricots and dates. In the Tibetan area — Gannan, traditional Tibetan foods such as zanba (or tsampa: roasted barley flour) and butter tea are the main foods. Don't worry if you can't get used to Chinese food or have special dietary requirements. Your guide will help arrange meals for you according to your preferences.
