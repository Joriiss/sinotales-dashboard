Chongqing is a city as unpredictable as it is captivating. Known as the "Mountainous City" for its jaw-dropping terrain and the "Foggy City" for those dreamy, almost magical mornings when mist rolls in, wrapping everything in a soft, mysterious glow. It's a place that will surprise you at every turn. If you're planning a trip to Chongqing, you've got to get familiar with its weather. The city's climate is as dramatic as its skyline. One minute you're sweating through a spicy hotpot meal in the summer heat, and the next, you're bundled up, sipping tea while the winter fog transforms the city into a scene from a movie. Timing your visit can make all the difference. Generally, the best times to visit Chongqing are spring (March to May) and autumn (September to October). Best Time for Fruit Picking in Chongqing Fruit picking is a fun and rewarding activity, perfect for enjoying the outdoors while tasting fresh, seasonal produce. Chongqing, with its diverse climate and fertile land, offers a variety of fruits throughout the year. Here's a guide to the best fruit-picking seasons in Chongqing: • Strawberries (December – May): Many farms in the suburbs offer strawberry picking, with peak season in early spring. • Loquats (April – June): Found in areas like Bishan and Jiangjin, these golden fruits ripen in late spring. • Cherries (April – June): The cherry orchards in Tongnan and Beibei are popular for picking during early summer. • Peaches (June – August): Various farms, especially in Dazu and Yongchuan, offer juicy peaches in mid-summer. • Grapes (July – September): Vineyards in Tongliang and Jiangbei have fresh grapes ready for picking. • Oranges & Citrus Fruits (October – January): Chongqing is famous for its citrus fruits, especially in Wanzhou and Fuling. The Worst Time to Visit Chongqing While Chongqing is a year-round destination, there are certain times when visiting can be less than ideal. Whether it's the sweltering summer heat, the rainy season, or the overwhelming crowds during holidays, knowing when not to visit can save you a lot of hassle. Hot Season (July to August) • Extreme Heat: Chongqing is famously known as one of China's "Four Furnaces." Temperatures often soar above 35°C (95°F), with humidity making it feel even hotter. • Heavy Rainfall: Summer is also the rainy season, with frequent thunderstorms that can disrupt outdoor plans. Visiting air-conditioned attractions like the Three Gorges Museum, Chongqing Science and Technology Museum, or the large shopping malls (which have plenty of shaded areas) can help you make the best of it. Chongqing's night markets and riverside bars come alive in the summer, and the cooler evenings are perfect for exploring the city's vibrant street food scene. Hongya Cave night market Rainy Season (May to September) • Frequent Downpours: The rainy season can bring sudden, heavy showers that last for hours, making outdoor activities difficult. • Humidity: The combination of rain and heat creates a sticky, uncomfortable atmosphere. On rainy days, exploring covered areas like Jiefangbei Pedestrian Street or the Chongqing Art Museum can help you make the best of it. Jiefangbei Pedestrian Street Chinese New Year (Late January to Mid-February) • Crowds: Chinese New Year is the busiest travel season in China. Trains, buses, and flights are packed, and popular attractions are overcrowded. • Higher Prices: Accommodation and transportation costs spike during this period. If you don't mind the crowds, Chinese New Year is a fantastic time to experience traditional celebrations, such as lantern festivals, dragon dances, and fireworks. Visiting lesser-known spots like Tongliang or Dazu can help you avoid the crowds. National Day Golden Week (October 1-7) • Overcrowding: National Day is one of China's biggest holidays, and Chongqing's top attractions, like the Three Natural Bridges and Hongya Cave, are packed with tourists. • Higher Costs: Like Chinese New Year, prices for accommodations and flights are significantly higher.
