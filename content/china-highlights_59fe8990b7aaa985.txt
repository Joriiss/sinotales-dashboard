Weather: March in Jiuzhaigou is a transition from winter to spring, with average temperatures ranging from 3–16°C (37–61°F). The weather can be unpredictable, and you may get snow, cloudy skies, or sunny days. Expect around half of the month to have rain or snow. Clothing: Bring a windproof jacket, a down coat, thermal leggings, a hat, gloves, and heat packs. Jiuzhaigou in March is still quite cold, especially in the morning and evening, so you need to keep warm. Is March Good to Visit Jiuzhaigou? I went to Jiuzhaigou in late March, and from my visiting experience, March is not really the best time in terms of scenery. It's the dry season, and many trees haven't grown new leaves yet. But if you prefer traveling when there are fewer people and lower prices, or if you want to experience the transition between winter and spring, March can still be worth considering. 1. Jiuzhaigou's Daily Visitors are around 5,000-13,000 in March (35,000+ during Peak Season) On the day we went, there were only around 13,000 visitors. We queued for about ten minutes at the entrance to get on the shuttle bus. Inside the park, it felt quite peaceful, and even at Nuorilang Center(the transfer hub and dining area), we didn't have to line up for food. When taking the shuttle buses between scenic spots, we almost always found seats. There weren't many people at Norilang Center and Five Flower Lake 2. Visiting in March Means a Later Start and a Relaxed Pace If you're visiting Jiuzhaigou in March, entering the park around 8:30 or later is perfectly fine. Unlike the peak season, you don't have to get up at 6 am just to make it in by 7:30. You'll still have plenty of time to explore, and unless you're doing a Tibetan-style photoshoot inside the park, you can easily cover most of the main sights. But you still need to dress warm. When we set out at 8:30 am, it was around 5°C (41°F), so gloves and a hat were necessary. By noon, it warmed up to above 20°C (68°F), and we could take off our jackets and just wear sweaters. 3. You Can Enjoy a Lower Budget for Tickets and Hotels. Both hotels and entrance tickets cost only about half of the peak season price. In March, you'll find snow scenery is mostly along the upper sections of the left route (Zechawa Valley) and the right route (Rize Valley), while peach blossoms are concentrated in the lower section of the Shuzheng Valley. Long Lake: The entire lake is still frozen in March, so you can enjoy the snow scenery even if it's not snowing. Jiuzhaigou, Long Lake Five-Color Pool: Snow covers the surrounding forest and lake area, though the water level is relatively low in March. Jiuzhaigou Valley, Five-Color Pool Panda Lake: On the uphill path to the viewing platform, you can see the side of an ice waterfall. You can only admire it from a distance, as the path to the waterfall was destroyed in the earthquake and is no longer accessible. Actually, apart from these spots, you can also see snowy mountain peaks from most other locations in Jiuzhaigou. Where to See Peach Blossoms Tiger Lake: You can enjoy the contrast of Tiffany-blue waters and pink peach blossoms. The peach blossoms were about to bloom in Tiger Lake when we went Reed Lake: Along the roadside and on the hills, you'll find plenty of blooming peach trees. This was where we saw the most peach blossoms. We saw blooming peach flowers in Reed Lake Shuzheng Village: Tibetan-style buildings, lakes, and peach blossoms come together, giving you a blend of culture and nature. Just note that the peach flowers usually bloom from late March to mid-April.
