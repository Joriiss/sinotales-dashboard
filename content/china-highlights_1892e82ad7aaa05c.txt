Weather: In April, Beijing experiences a noticeable rise in temperatures, ranging from 10°C to 20°C (50-68°F), with occasional highs up to 27°C (81°F). Days are warm and pleasant, while mornings and evenings can still feel a bit cool. You may encounter light dust in the air, but these are usually short-lived and won't affect your trip. In mid to late April, willow catkins — fluffy white seeds from trees — appear in some areas. For most people, they are just a seasonal sign of spring, though if you have pollen allergies or allergic rhinitis, it's something to note. What to wear: In early April, a sweatshirt or light sweater is usually enough, while later in the month you could switch to long sleeves or even short sleeves with a light jacket. Since spring in Beijing is often windy, bringing a windbreaker is a smart choice. If you do encounter dust or pollen, bringing a mask can make you more comfortable. Is April a Good Time to Go to Beijing? Yes, April is one of the best times to visit Beijing. The temperatures are moderate, with the chill of winter gone and the heat of summer yet to arrive. Aside from small seasonal changes, the city is full of spring vitality, offering good conditions for all kinds of tourist activities. April is also the season when flowers are in full bloom, ideal for photography and flower viewing. Walking through parks, hutongs, or ancient courtyards, you'll feel the vibrancy of spring, adding color and joy to your trip. Additionally, compared to the busier months of May and June, April is less crowded, so you could enjoy a more relaxed pace while exploring the city's history, culture, and scenic beauty. What to Enjoy in Beijing During April Here are some highlight activities in Beijing for April. Discover Beijing's Blossoms If you visit Beijing in April, don't miss this prime month for flower viewing. Here are three must-visit spots to include in your itinerary. The Forbidden City is a top choice. In early April, you could admire crabapple blossoms at Yihe Xuan and Wenhua Hall, see Chinese flowering plum in the Imperial Garden, and view flowering peaches near the Gate of Divine Might. Later in the month, don't miss the vibrant wisteria at Yonghe Palace, which beautifully contrasts with the ancient palace walls. The Forbidden City in spring In the Summer Palace, the Le Shou Hall hosts two magnolia trees in early April, one of which was transplanted by Emperor Qianlong from the south 200 years ago. Along the imperial road on the back hill of Longevity Hill, over a hundred lilac trees form a charming lilac path. Visiting in early April is best for capturing expansive floral scenes. By the end of the month, there are fewer flowers, but the spring atmosphere remains delightful. Magnolias in Leshou Hall Yuyuantan Park is also worth a half-day visit. In early April, you could see cherry blossoms near the North Gate; later in the month, head to the 500-meter wisteria corridor near the West Gate. Cherry blossoms at Yuyuantan A Spring Day on the Great Wall In spring, flowers bloom not only in the city but also in the mountains away from the hustle and bustle. The Great Wall is an ideal place to experience this springtime beauty. The peak bloom period on the Great Wall is in early to mid-April. By late April, there are fewer flowers, but the scenery is still stunning. For those interested in a leisurely visit or a family outing, the Mutianyu section is perfect. It offers cable cars, chairlifts, and toboggan rides, with gentle paths for easy walking. For those seeking a physical challenge or a sense of adventure, the Jiankou section is a great choice.
