It has beautiful green trees and a calm atmosphere, making it a great spot for both locals and visitors to relax after visiting the Forbidden City. One special thing about the park is its ancient cypress trees, some of which are over 600 years old. These tall trees provide shade and peace, and their twisted branches and trunks make the park even more charming. • Panoramic Views of the Forbidden City Standing in the famous Wanchun Pavilion in Jing Shan Park, you can enjoy stunning panoramic views of the Forbidden City and the surrounding area on clear days. Wanchun Pavilion is the best spot for taking photos. Our guide David looked at the panoramic view of the Forbidden City in Jingshan Park • A Great Place to Learn About Chinese Feng Shui (风水) As mentioned earlier, the hill is man-made. You may wonder why an artificial hill was constructed behind the Forbidden City during the Ming dynasty. The ultimate reason lies in Feng Shui. Feng Shui, which translates to "wind-water" in English, is an ancient Chinese practice that involves arranging the environment to harmonize with the natural world and promote the flow of positive energy, or "qi." It is based on the Taoist understanding of nature, particularly the belief that the land is alive and filled with energy. According to Feng Shui theory, an ideal site for Forbidden City building a house features water in front and a mountain or hill behind. This is why a stream was constructed in front of Tian'anmen, with Jing Shan Hill located behind the Forbidden City. • Appreciate Its Historical Significance You can explore the most significant tree where the last Ming emperor committed suicide by hanging himself. From Wanchun Pavilion, you can overlook the entire Forbidden City, as well as see the White Pagoda in Beihai Park, the Drum Tower, the Bell Tower, the Olympic Tower, and other iconic buildings in Beijing. During these months, you can see beautiful landscapes and colorful flowers, like peonies and Chinese roses, all in bloom. Flowers in Jinshan park However, there are some good and bad things to consider. In peak season, you might have to wait in long lines at the ticket office. If you want to avoid the crowds, visiting from November to February is a great idea. You can also enjoy a stunning view of the Forbidden City, especially on snowy days, from December to January. Panorama of the Forbidden City in the snow Unlike many tourist spots in Beijing, you can buy tickets right at the park without needing to book online. The entrance fee is only CNY 2, and the park is open every day from 6:30 AM to 8:00 PM. It's usually better to enjoy the view during the day rather than at night. If you visit on Fridays, Saturdays, or public holidays, you might want to go to the top of Jing Shan Hill in the evening to see the night view. From Jing Shan Park, you can see the Shenwumen of the Forbidden City lit up, usually between 6:30 PM and 7:30 PM. How to Get to Jing Shan Park The best way to get to Jing Shan Park is to walk a few minutes from the north gate of the Forbidden City. An underground tunnel connects the north gate of the Forbidden City to the entrance of the park. Vehicles, including taxis, are not allowed to park between the park and the city, and there is no adjacent metro station. A Classic City Walk including Jingshan Park • Route: Shichahai Lake - Yinding Bridge - Houhai Bar Street - Beihai Park - Jingshan Park - Forbidden City • Duration: 2-3 hours This route is a feast for the eyes. Start at Shichahai Lake, a picturesque area surrounded by ancient architecture and lush greenery. You can rent a boat or simply stroll along the lake's edges. Yinding Bridge, with its elegant arch, is a great spot for photos. Next, explore the bars and cafes lining Houhai Bar Street. Beihai Park, with its tranquil lakes and ancient pavilions, offers a peaceful retreat. As you climb Jingshan Mountain, you'll be rewarded with a breathtaking view of the Forbidden City. This walk is perfect for those who love beautiful scenery and want to capture stunning photos. The golden ginkgo leaves on the ground and the red walls create an intoxicating atmosphere.
