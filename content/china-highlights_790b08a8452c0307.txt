We'd be happy to help you customize and adjust your itinerary. Best Time to Visit Beijing The best times to visit Beijing are spring (April to May) and autumn (September to early November). These seasons offer the most pleasant weather and the most scenic experiences. In spring, Beijing warms up with blooming flowers and fresh greenery. You could visit the Yuyuantan park to see cherry and begonia blossoms or walk the Great Wall surrounded by soft spring mist. It's a lively, refreshing season to explore the city's gardens and temples. In autumn, the city is at its most beautiful. The skies are clear and the air is crisp. You'll see golden ginkgo trees in the Forbidden City, red maple leaves in Fragrant Hills, and enjoy the Great Wall at its most photogenic. This is also the best time for outdoor walks and photography. The Forbidden City in Autumn Winter (December to February) is low season, but it has its own charm. You could enjoy fewer crowds, discounted hotel rates, and see Beijing covered in snow if you're lucky. A fun winter highlight is skiing at Nanshan Ski Resort. It is a great option for beginners and families looking for an exciting getaway. Just prepare for dry and chilly air. We don't recommend summer (July to August) due to heat, humidity, and crowds. However, early June and late August are better windows if summer is your only option as they're slightly cooler and less packed. Also, try to avoid the May Day holiday (May 1—3) and National Day Golden Week (October 1—7). These are China's peak travel periods, with heavy crowds at all major attractions and harder ticket availability. See detailed information on Where to Stay in Beijing. How to Travel to/around Beijing This section will help you know how to travel to and around Beijing. As the capital of China, Beijing is one of the country's main international gateways. It has two major airports: • Beijing Capital International Airport (PEK): It is about 1 hour from the city center and is used by many traditional international airlines. • Beijing Daxing International Airport (PKX): A new and beautifully designed airport, about 1.5 hours from downtown, with fewer international flights. Direct flights connect Beijing to major cities across Asia, Europe, North America, and Oceania. If you're planning a multi-city China tour, Beijing is a great starting or ending point, with high-speed trains and short flights to Shanghai, Xi'an, Chengdu, and more. If you are taking an international cruise ship, you could arrive at Tianjin International Cruise Home Port and then go to Beijing, which usually takes 3 hours by car. What to Eat and Where to Eat in Beijing Beijing is not only rich in history but also full of unforgettable flavors. Of course, Beijing roast duck is the must-try classic. But beyond that, the city offers a world of authentic local dishes worth exploring. You could warm up with a traditional copper-pot lamb hotpot, bite into a flaky Doornail Meat Pie, or slurp up a bowl of zhajiangmian (noodles with savory soybean paste). If you have a sweet tooth, don't miss the tanghulu (colorful candied haws on a stick), a favorite local street treat. For something more unusual, you might try douzhi (fermented mung bean juice) and luzhu huoshao (pork stew with bread). Their flavors may surprise you, but they are long-time favorites among Beijing locals. Douzhi, jiaoquan and xiancai For the best street food experiences, head to Nanluoguxiang, Huguosi Snack Street, or Qianmen Street, where time-honored flavors meet lively local life. Whether you're snacking or dining, these places are great for tasting Beijing in bite-sized form. Explore the food and street food markets of Beijing in our Top 10 Must-Try Beijing Foods and Top 5 Beijing Snack Streets. Is Beijing Wheelchair-Friendly? Yes. Beijing is increasingly accessible, and with the right planning, it can be a rewarding and comfortable destination, even if you or someone in your group uses a wheelchair. Many of the city's top attractions offer wheelchair-friendly access. The Forbidden City has smooth walkways and ramp access at key spots. Some halls still involve stairs, so companions might be helpful to navigate the more challenging areas. The Temple of Heaven and Summer Palace also have designated accessible routes. At the Summer Palace, you could even enjoy a relaxing boat ride on Kunming Lake, assisted by on-site staff. It's a great way to take in the scenery without walking long distances. Our guests in wheelchairs visited the Temple of Heaven As for the Great Wall, most sections are too steep, but Juyongguan (Juyong Pass) is a rare exception. It offers a flat, paved area and ramp access at the base, allowing wheelchair users to enjoy the ancient atmosphere without climbing. Getting around Beijing with a wheelchair is possible but may require extra planning. While central sidewalks are wide and curb ramps are common, public transport like subways and taxis may still pose some inconvenience.
