Tibet is open for travelling. As one of the most fascinating places in China, it's known for the dramatic mountains, ancient monasteries, and unique culture. However, independent foreign visitors are not allowed to explore Tibet on their own. You must book through a licensed travel agency and be accompanied by a local guide. In this guide, we'll walk you through everything you need to know to plan a comfortable and well-structured Tibet tour. • What Documents Do I Need for Visiting Tibet? • How to Get to Tibet • How Many Days to Spend in Tibet? • Best Time to Visit Tibet • Where to Stay in Tibet • Visiting Tips What Documents Do I Need for Visiting Tibet? To visit Tibet, the most important document is the Tibet Travel Permit (Tibet Entry Permit), which you must apply for through a licensed travel agency before arrival. With this permit, you can explore Lhasa and many nearby areas. If you plan to travel further, such as to Shigatse, Nyingchi, Ngari, or Everest Base Camp, you will also need additional permits. These may include the Aliens' Travel Permit, issued by the local Public Security Bureau, and in some cases a Military Permit for sensitive border regions. There's no need to worry about handling the paperwork yourself. Travel with us, and we will arrange all necessary permits once you provide a copy of your passport and Chinese visa. There are two main ways to reach Tibet: from China's mainland or from Nepal. Entering from China is the easiest and most convenient option for most travelers. From China's Mainland - Most Easiest There are direct flights from China's major cities to Lhasa in Tibet, such as from Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu, and Chongqing. The most frequent and reliable flights to Tibet are from Chengdu, with about eight flights per day. Trains to Tibet depart from these cities: Beijing, Shanghai, Guangzhou, Xi'an, Chongqing, Lanzhou, and Xining. Xining is the best place to start the train journey, as it is the beginning point of the most beautiful section of the Qinghai-Tibet Railway with the shortest train journey to Lhasa (20–22 hours), compared to around 30–50 hours for further departure points. Qinghai-Tibet Railway Tip: Taking a flight into Tibet is better than a train because a good sleep in a hotel in Lhasa is much more helpful to altitude acclimation than spending a night on the train. Most passengers find it more difficult to fall asleep on the train, and tiredness makes altitude sickness more uncomfortable. Book an international flight from a major U.S. city to a gateway city in China, such as Beijing, Shanghai, or Guangzhou. 2. Spend 1–2 days in your arrival city to recover from jet lag and enjoy some sightseeing. 3. Take a domestic flight to Chengdu, the most convenient hub for flights to Tibet. Almost all flights to Lhasa transit through Chengdu. You can stay overnight in Chengdu, and you'll have chance to see China's giant pandas or try local Sichuan food. 4. From Chengdu, take a morning flight to Lhasa (about 2½ hours). Arriving earlier in the day gives you time to rest and acclimatize to the altitude before bedtime, which helps reduce the risk of altitude sickness. 5. For your return journey, the simplest option is to fly back from Lhasa Gonggar Airport to another major Chinese city or your next destination, and then connect with your international flight home. This route is usually the fastest and most reliable way to travel out of Tibet. You can also enter Tibet from Kathmandu, either by flight or overland via the Gyirong border (possibly with a convenient side tour to Mount Everest). However, when traveling to Tibet from Nepal, you should first consider issues of a China visa and Tibet Entry Permit, no matter whether you'll go by flight or road. Procedures are different from traveling from a Chinese mainland city to Tibet. You must apply for a China visa and Tibet permits in Nepal via a travel agent. Any China visa issued in your resident country will be invalid for Tibet travel. You will need to stay in Kathmandu for at least 3 working days to wait for the visa and permit. You can check more details at How to Travel from Nepal. How Many Days to Spend in Tibet? For most first-time visitors, a 7–8 days trip to Tibet is ideal. It balances classical spots, cultural depth and natural beauty, while giving your body time to adjust to the altitude. However, the length of your Tibet trip depends on your interests and physical condition. Here are some common options to help you plan: 4–5 Days: Lhasa and Yamdrok Lake This is the classic short trip within and around Lhasa. You'll have time to adjust to the high altitude, and explore the famous Potala Palace, meet devout pilgrims at Jokhang Temple, and see the monks debate at Sera Monestery. Outside the city, a drive to Yamdrok Lake brings you to shimmering turquoise waters framed by snowcapped peaks. This option may take half a day to a full day. It is perfect if you want a quick yet unforgettable taste of both culture and nature. 7–8 Days: Add Everest Base Camp (EBC) or Nyingchi With a week in Tibet, you can go beyond Lhasa. Traveling west, the road to Everest Base Camp takes you across high passes and reach Rongbuk Monastery, the world's highest temple. A night here lets you wake up to sunrise over Mount Everest — a moment travelers never forget. This vast, untamed land is home to Mount Kailash, Lake Manasarovar, Zanda Tulin, and rugged highland scenery that feels worlds away from modern life. Longer itineraries also mean more free time and flexible trip. Since travel in Tibet often involves many hours on the road, extra relaxing time will makes your journey more comfortable. Best Time to Visit Tibet Generally, the best time to visit Tibet is from May to October. However, the ideal period depends on your interests—whether you want to trek, admire plateau landscapes, or immerse yourself in Tibetan festivals. The weather in Tibet is largely affected by altitude. Therefore, even in the same month, the temperature in southern (low altitude) and northern (high altitude) Tibet can be very different. Every season has its advantages and characteristics. Exploring Tibet in different months shows you different scenery and cultural activities for different travel experiences. • Spring (April–May): Pleasant weather, fewer crowds, and lower hotel prices (except the May 1–5 holiday). Nyingchi's peach blossoms are a highlight. • Summer (June–August): The best time for seniors and families, as oxygen levels are higher. It's usually the rainy season. Nature is lush, local festivals are abundant, and you can even join Tibetans in barley harvesting or yak herding. Expect bigger crowds and higher prices for flights and hotels. • Autumn (September–October): The clearest skies and most stable weather, ideal for trekking to Everest Base Camp or Mount Kailash. This is the best time to see Mount Everest's snowcapped peak under blue, cloudless skies. • Winter (November–March): Quiet and more affordable. Many Tibetans make pilgrimage journeys to Lhasa, creating a deeply spiritual atmosphere. Unique winter sights include glowing blue glaciers and frozen lakes. For more details, see Best Times to Visit Tibet. Where to Stay in Tibet Tibet is remote, high-altitude, and has limited infrastructure. Hotels are fewer, more expensive, and often below the standard of big Chinese cities. Foreign travelers can only stay at certified hotels, so options are further restricted. Transportation within Tibet Traveling around Tibet is mainly done by private car. The drive can be long, but the road journeys are part of the experience. You'll see snow-capped mountains, rivers, lakes, and vast grasslands along the way. On our private tours, we design routes to minimize time on the road, and you'll have a comfortable vehicle with a guide accompanying you throughout the trip. There are also train connections from Lhasa to Shigatse, Nyingchi, Shannan, and Nagqu, which can be a effective alternative. However, if you travel by train, our private car and guide may not accompany you the whole way, since vehicles are arranged from Lhasa. Flights are available between Lhasa, Shigatse, and Nyingchi, but they are limited in number and not always reliable. Budget for a Trip to Tibet The cost of traveling in Tibet depends on factors such as season, tour length, group size, hotel standard, and whether you choose a private or group tour. As a reference, an 8-day private tour for two people costs around USD 2,200 per person, covering transfers, meals, entrance fees, hotels, and guiding. To save money, consider booking a few months in advance to secure early discounts, or travel with four or more people to share private tour costs. Winter (December–January) is the most budget-friendly season, with reduced prices on flights, hotels, and entrance tickets. Visiting Tibet is Safe, But Be Prepared for Altitude Sickness Tibet is generally very safe, with a low crime rate and a reliable security measures, especially in Lhasa. The real challenge for most travelers is altitude sickness, as the average elevation is around 4,500 meters (15,000 feet). Most visitors, including seniors and kids, can travel safely, but it's wise to consult a doctor if you have heart or blood pressure concerns. Here are some practical methods to help you to ease the altitude sickness: 1. To help your body adjust, avoid washing your hair or taking long showers during your first 2–3 days in Lhasa, and steer clear of strenuous activities such as running, jumping, or climbing steep stairs. 2. If you plan to visit higher-altitude spots like Yamdrok Lake or Everest Base Camp, spend at least 3–4 days in Lhasa first. 3. Oxygen therapy can ease discomfort, especially at night, when good sleep is crucial for acclimatization. 4. Wearing a warm hat helps protect your head from cold winds and may reduce headaches.
