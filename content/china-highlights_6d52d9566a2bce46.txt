China's Dragon Boat Festival is referred to as Duanwu Jie ('Start-of the-Fifth-Solar-Month Festival') or Tuen Ng Festival (in Cantonese areas). Dragon boat races and rice dumplings are two of the most distinctive elements of the festival. In 2026, Dragon Boat Festival falls on Friday, June 19. Dragon Boat Festival's date varies from year to year on the Gregorian calendar, falling in June most years or in late May. Dragon Boat Festival is celebrated on the fifth day of the fifth month on the Chinese Lunar Calendar, so the festival is often called the 'double fifth' festival. Why is it celebrated on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month? Dragon Boat festival is celebrated in remembrance of Qu Yuan, a Chinese poet and statesman who drowned himself in a river on the fifth day of the fifth lunar month (June 27, 278 BC) in protest of his beloved homeland's fall. Learn more about the story of Qu Yuan. Is Dragon Boat Festival a Public Holiday? Dragon Boat Festival is a public holiday in China. In Chinese mainland, it is a 3-day national holiday including weekend, the same as in Hong Kong, Macau and Taiwan. This annual event, with its roots in the Chinese mainland, has spread to Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan. Now, it is also widely celebrated in other Asian countries, such as Singapore and Malaysia, as well as many other cities in the world, such as Denver in the U.S., Vancouver in Canada, and Wellington in New Zealand. During this festival, people gather together to enjoy various cultural activities and traditions.
